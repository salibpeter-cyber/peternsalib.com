I work on the law of transformative AI. My work investigates how law and legal institutions can help to reduce catastrophic and other societal-scale risks from advanced AI. One strand of that work argues that, in addition to technical alignment, humanity should shape AIs’ behavior using legal and economic incentives. This means giving AI systems themselves certain legal rights and duties, for the sake of governing them, much as we do with other non-human agents—like corporations and nation-states.

I also write about optimal safety regulation for the AI industry, the constitutional barriers to such regulation, AI geopolitics, new approaches to technical AI alignment, and how the post-AGI economy will affect ordinary humans.

My scholarship appears in the University of Chicago, Virginia, Michigan, Northwestern, Texas, and Washington University law reviews, and in peer-reviewed venues including AIES and AI & Society. My book on AI rights, co-authored with Simon Goldstein, is forthcoming with Cambridge University Press.

I am the W. Ronald Robins Professor of Law and Associate Professor of Law (with tenure) at the University of Houston Law Center, and Founder and Executive Co-Director of the Center for Law & AI Risk. I am also a Law and Policy Advisor to the Center for AI Safety, a Visiting Senior Fellow at the Institute for Law & AI, a Senior Visiting Scholar at Forethought, and a Contributing Editor at Lawfare. Before teaching I was a Climenko Fellow at Harvard Law School, practiced at Sidley Austin, and clerked for Judge Frank H. Easterbrook. You can reach me at psalib@central.uh.edu.
